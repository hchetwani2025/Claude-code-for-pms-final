What happened, plainly

Three facts explain the "phone never rings" tickets.

  1. 4.2 cut the time to answer an offer from 90 to 60 seconds. More offers ran out before people reached their phones.
  2. The code counts a missed offer the same as a "no". Each one takes 0.12 off a responder's score. A "yes" gives back only 0.08. Nothing restores the score over time.
  3. 4.2 also cut the weight of that score from 0.40 to 0.25. But for anyone who hit the floor, the score was still enough to rank them last. So four responders stopped getting offers and could not earn their way back.

What it was not

Not only the August dip, and not the proximity change on its own. The average fell and then recovered. The four responders did not recover.

What it means

Reverting 4.2 moves the pain to another group (Priya's warning). The fix is to stop treating a missed window as a refusal, and to let the score recover.

The mechanism

A loop with no way out

Each turn of the loop makes the next turn more likely. Nothing in the code breaks it.

Why this settles it

The code knows the difference between a "no" and a missed offer, and then drops it on the next line. The 60-second window creates more missed offers. The score has a floor at 0 and no reset.

How the skill stops this happening again

4.2 would have failed every check

Each check below matches something that went wrong with 4.2, in Rook's own documents.

1
Owner named · missed

Nobody owned the question

Marcus asked on 14 Aug whether the change should apply to people who turn work down. Wen was on PTO, and no one else answered. The roadmap lists each 4.2 item with the driver "Internal" and names no person.

2
Success measure · missed

"Went clean" was the bar

The only verdict on 13 Aug was "no rollback, no overnight pages". The headline average then recovered to 73%, while four responders stayed near zero. Nothing measured them.

3
Scope holds · missed

Two changes, and one item gone

The reweight and the timeout cut shipped together, so no one could separate their effects. "Availability Confidence" was committed to 4.2, but it is not in the 4.2 release notes, and no one says it was dropped.

4
Problem before fix · missed

The timeout cut stated no problem

The reweight named its problem: wide-geography responders were losing callouts. "Ping timeout tuning" gave only its effect: "Offers that go unanswered move to the next responder sooner." It never said who was hurting.
